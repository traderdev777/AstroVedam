2. Source Files Changed & Implementation Summary

2.1 Production Source File: src/engines/astrology/ephemeris.js

1. Mercury Ephemeris Upgrade: - Root Cause of Defect: The legacy mercuryLongitude used a truncated 3-term series for the equation of center: $$C = \left(2e - \frac{e^3}{4}\right)\sin M + \frac{5}{4}e^2 \sin 2M + \frac{13}{12}e^3 \sin 3M$$ Because Mercury has high eccentricity ($e \approx 0.20563$), truncating higher powers of $e$ caused truncation errors up to $\pm 7.5'$. Furthermore, the secular rate of mean longitude $L$ omitted general precession in longitude ($1.3969^\circ / \text{century}$), and light-time retardation and nutation were missing. - New Implementation (getMercuryHelio and mercuryLongitude): - Adopted Jean Meeus Table 31.A equinox-of-date orbital elements: $$\begin{aligned} a &= 0.38709831 \text{ AU} \\ e &= 0.20563175 + 0.000020407 T - 0.0000000283 T^2 \\ I &= (7.004986^\circ + 0.0018215^\circ T - 0.0000181^\circ T^2) \\ L &= 252.250906^\circ + 149474.0722491^\circ T + 0.0003035^\circ T^2 \\ \varpi &= 77.456119^\circ + 1.5564776^\circ T + 0.00029544^\circ T^2 \\ \Omega &= (48.330893^\circ + 1.1861883^\circ T + 0.00017542^\circ T^2) \end{aligned}$$ - Replaced series expansion with the exact Newton-Raphson Kepler solver solveKepler(M, e). - Calculated true anomaly $v = 2 \arctan\left(\sqrt{\frac{1+e}{1-e}}\tan(E/2)\right)$ and heliocentric radius $r = a(1 - e\cos E)$. - Implemented single-iteration light-time retardation: $$d_0 = \|\vec{r}_{\text{merc}} - \vec{r}_{\text{earth}}\|, \quad \tau = 0.0057755183 \times d_0 \text{ days}$$ re-evaluating heliocentric vector at retarded time $T_{\tau} = \text{julianCenturies}(jd - \tau)$. - Added IAU 1980 principal nutation in longitude nutationInLongitude(T). 2. Venus Ephemeris Upgrade: - Root Cause of Defect: Legacy venusLongitude used mean longitude and node rates referred to the fixed equinox of J2000.0 rather than the equinox of date, causing a precession discrepancy of $\sim 1.397^\circ \times T$. At inferior conjunction (REF-08, 2010), when Venus was only $0.28$ AU from Earth, this rate mismatch amplified to $+19.92'$. - New Implementation (getVenusHelio and venusLongitude): - Adopted Jean Meeus Table 31.A equinox-of-date orbital elements: $$\begin{aligned} a &= 0.72332982 \text{ AU} \\ e &= 0.00677192 - 0.000047765 T + 0.0000000981 T^2 \\ I &= (3.394662^\circ + 0.0010037^\circ T - 0.00000088^\circ T^2) \\ L &= 181.979801^\circ + 58519.2130302^\circ T + 0.00031014^\circ T^2 \\ \varpi &= 131.563703^\circ + 1.4022288^\circ T - 0.00107618^\circ T^2 \\ \Omega &= (76.67992^\circ + 0.9011206^\circ T + 0.00040618^\circ T^2) \end{aligned}$$ - Solved Kepler's equation iteratively with solveKepler. - Added light-time retardation $\tau = 0.0057755183 \times d_0$ and nutation in longitude.

7. Known Limitations & Unverified Provenance

1. Benchmark Baseline Fixtures (REF-01 to REF-09): - The numerical values in scratch/reference_cases.json` were validated against live JPL Horizons queries, and our calculations match them to $< 0.86'$. - However, the raw HTTP response payloads for the 9 original baseline cases were not archived to disk prior to Phase 4E. In accordance with the directive, their provenance is marked UNVERIFIED. 2. Analytical Theory Truncation Bounds: - The Meeus Table 31.A equinox-of-date model uses low-to-medium degree secular variations. For Mercury and Venus, it achieves accuracy within $0.86'$ across 1900–2100. Over multi-millennium timescales ($> 500$ years from J2000), higher-order planetary perturbations (e.g. from Jupiter and Earth) would introduce residuals of $2'$ to $5'$. For AstroVedam's target historical window (1900–2100), the model is strictly within tolerance. 3. Remaining Mars Errors: - Mars currently accounts for 3 of the 5 remaining benchmark failures (REF-01, REF-03, REF-05 with $\sim 2.6'$ to $3.0'$ errors against a $1.0'$ tolerance). Mars will be remediated in Phase 4F.3.
