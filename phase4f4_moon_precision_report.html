3. ROOT CAUSE EVIDENCE & DIAGNOSTIC TRACE (PHASE GATE 2)

An exhaustive trace of the Moon calculation path in src/engines/astrology/ephemeris.js (moonLongitude, lines 76–115) and downstream consumers in src/engines/astrology/kundali.js revealed five distinct analytical omissions in the legacy engine:

1. Truncation of 44 Periodic Terms (Meeus Table 47.A)

The legacy implementation only evaluated 16 periodic terms in longitude $\Sigma l$. In Jean Meeus' Astronomical Algorithms (Chapter 47, Table 47.A, pages 339–340), the full truncated ELP-2000/82 series contains 60 periodic terms. The omitted 44 terms have individual amplitudes reaching $10,675 \times 10^{-6}$ degrees ($0.64'$)—for example: - Term 17: $4D - M'$ ($+10,675 \times 10^{-6}°$) - Term 18: $3M'$ ($+10,034 \times 10^{-6}°$) - Term 19: $4D - 2M'$ ($+8,548 \times 10^{-6}°$) - Term 20: $2D + M - M'$ ($-7,888 \times 10^{-6}°$) - Term 21: $2D + M$ ($-6,766 \times 10^{-6}°$) In the failing REF-02 scenario (April 13, 2000, 11:45 UTC), these omitted terms were coherently phase-aligned, producing an accumulated omitted longitude perturbation of $-1.91'$.

2. Omission of Earth Orbital Eccentricity Multiplier ($E$)

In gravitational perturbation theory, terms involving the Sun's mean anomaly $M$ represent solar perturbations modulated by Earth's orbital eccentricity $e$. Meeus specifies that: $$E = 1 - 0.002516 T - 0.0000074 T^2$$ - Periodic terms with $|M| = 1$ must be multiplied by $E$. - Periodic terms with $|M| = 2$ must be multiplied by $E^2$. The legacy engine evaluated all terms with constant unity coefficients, omitting secular damping of solar eccentricity perturbations.

3. Complete Omission of IAU 1980 Nutation in Longitude ($\Delta\psi$)

In apparent geocentric astronomy, apparent ecliptic longitude is referred to the true equinox of date, requiring the addition of the nutation in longitude: $$\Delta\psi \approx -17.20'' \sin(\Omega) - 1.32'' \sin(2L_0)$$ This correction fluctuates with an 18.6-year period up to $\pm 17.2''$ ($\pm 0.287'$). The legacy Moon function omitted nutation entirely, introducing a systematic equinox offset.

4. Omission of Planetary Additive Perturbations

Gravitational perturbations from Venus and Jupiter introduce additive longitude terms (Meeus 1998, eq. 47.6, p. 341–342): $$A_1 = 119.75° + 131.849° T$$ $$A_2 = 53.09° + 479264.290° T$$ $$\delta l_{\text{planetary}} = 3958 \sin(A_1) + 1962 \sin(L' - F) + 318 \sin(A_2) \quad (10^{-6}°)$$ These terms contribute up to $0.24'$ of additional longitude correction.

5. Absence of Input Safety Guard

Legacy moonLongitude(jd) lacked finite numeric guards, causing NaN propagation on degenerate inputs.
