3. Mathematical & Theoretical Derivation

3.1 Coordinate Frame Reconciliation

NASA JPL Horizons Quantity 31 computes Observer Ecliptic Longitude and Latitude (ObsEcLon, ObsEcLat) defined in the IAU 1976/1980 ecliptic and equinox of date. The physical pipeline for an apparent planetary position involves: `` r_apparent = r_geometric(t - tau) + Delta_r_aberration(t) + Delta_r_nutation(t) `

Where:

1. tau = d / c is the planetary light-time travel retardation (0.0057755183 days/AU). 2. r_geometric(t - tau) is Saturn's heliocentric position evaluated at the retarded epoch t - tau, subtracted from Earth's heliocentric position at epoch t. 3. Delta_lambda_nutation = Delta_psi is the IAU 1980 principal nutation in longitude. 4. Delta_lambda_aberration is the observer stellar aberration due to Earth's orbital velocity around the Sun.

3.2 Observer Stellar Aberration Formulation

Following Jean Meeus (Astronomical Algorithms, 2nd Ed., Chapter 23, "Stellar Aberration", p. 151) and the Explanatory Supplement to the Astronomical Almanac (Section 3.25):

The principal stellar aberration in ecliptic longitude is:

` Delta_lambda_aberr = -k * cos(lambda_Sun - lambda_planet) / cos(beta_planet) `

Where:

- k = 20.49552" = (20.49552 / 3600) deg ≈ 0.0056932 deg is the standard IAU constant of aberration. - lambda_Sun is the true geometric longitude of the Sun at epoch t. - lambda_planet is Saturn's geocentric light-time retarded longitude. - beta_planet = arcsin(z_Saturn / d) is Saturn's geocentric ecliptic latitude.

Verification of Mathematical Properties:

- Quadrature (lambda_Sun - lambda_planet = ±90°): cos(±90°) = 0 => Delta_lambda_aberr = 0. - Conjunction (lambda_Sun - lambda_planet = 0°): cos(0°) = 1 => Delta_lambda_aberr = -k / cos(beta) ≈ -20.5". - Opposition (lambda_Sun - lambda_planet = 180°): cos(180°) = -1 => Delta_lambda_aberr = +k / cos(beta) ≈ +20.5".

3.3 Astronomical Time-Scale Architecture

Planetary theories (VSOP87D, DE405, DE441) are parameterized strictly by uniform dynamical time: Terrestrial Time (TT) or Barycentric Dynamical Time (TDB). Civil calendars and user birth timestamps provide Universal Time (UTC). 1. TT vs TDB Equivalence: The relativistic transformation between TT and TDB consists of small periodic terms with peak amplitude < 0.00166 s (1.66 ms). Saturn's mean orbital motion is 0.0335 deg/day = 0.00139"/second. An offset of 1.66 ms corresponds to 2.3 x 10^-6 arcseconds, which is four orders of magnitude beneath our tolerance. Therefore, TDB is practically identical to TT for planetary longitudes at this precision level. 2. UTC to TT Conversion: ` JD_TT = JD_UTC + Delta_T / 86400 ` Where Delta_T = TT - UTC in seconds. 3. Modern Era (1972-01-01 to Present): ` Delta_T = (TAI - UTC) + 32.184 s ` Looked up from the authoritative IERS leap-second history (28 discrete leap seconds). At J2000.0 (2000-01-01 12:00 UTC), TAI - UTC = 32 s, yielding exactly Delta_T = 64.184 s. 4. Future Dates Beyond Latest Leap Second (2017+): Following NASA JPL Horizons' official operational specification ("the last known leap-second is used as a constant over future intervals"), dates beyond 2017 hold TAI - UTC = 37 s => Delta_T = 69.184 s. 5. Historical Pre-1972 Eras: Pre-1972 timestamps are evaluated using NASA TP-2006-214141 (Espenak & Meeus 2006) piecewise polynomials spanning 2000 BCE to 1972 CE, reflecting empirical historical Earth deceleration.
