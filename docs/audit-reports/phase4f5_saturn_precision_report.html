2.1 Orbital Dynamics & The Great Inequality

Saturn orbits the Sun at a mean heliocentric distance $a \approx 9.5549$ AU with an orbital period of $P_S \approx 29.457$ Julian years. Jupiter orbits at $a \approx 5.2026$ AU with $P_J \approx 11.862$ Julian years. The orbital mean motions have a near-commensurability of $5 : 2$: $$5 n_S - 2 n_J \approx 5(1221.5^\circ/\text{century}) - 2(3034.7^\circ/\text{century}) \approx 38.3^\circ/\text{century} = 0.383^\circ/\text{year}$$ This produces a fundamental gravitational mutual secular resonance known historically as the Great Inequality, with a super-period of: $$T_{\text{res}} = \frac{360^\circ}{0.383^\circ/\text{year}} \approx 940 \text{ years (observed empirical period } \approx 918 \text{ years)}$$

2.2 Mathematical Breakdown of the Pre-Upgrade 5-Term Model

In the baseline src/engines/astrology/ephemeris.js, Saturn heliocentric coordinates were computed via Paul Schlyter's (1979) empirical formulation: $$\Delta L = 0.812^\circ \sin(2M_J - 5M_S - 67.6^\circ) - 0.229^\circ \cos(2M_J - 4M_S - 2^\circ) + 0.119^\circ \sin(M_J - 2M_S - 3^\circ) + 0.046^\circ \sin(2M_J - 6M_S - 69^\circ) + 0.014^\circ \sin(M_J - 3M_S + 32^\circ)$$

At the REF-06 epoch (2023-06-21 16:00 UTC, $T = 0.2347205$ Julian centuries from J2000.0):

- Jupiter mean anomaly: $M_J = 19.8950^\circ + 3034.6906^\circ \times 0.2347205 \equiv 12.19^\circ$ - Saturn mean anomaly: $M_S = 316.9670^\circ + 1221.5354^\circ \times 0.2347205 \equiv 243.68^\circ$ - Phase argument of the dominant Great Inequality term: $$\phi_1 = 2M_J - 5M_S - 67.6^\circ = 2(12.19^\circ) - 5(243.68^\circ) - 67.6^\circ \equiv 178.42^\circ$$ - Because $\phi_1 \approx 178.42^\circ$, $\sin(\phi_1) \approx \sin(180^\circ - 1.58^\circ) \approx 0.0275$. - Consequently, the primary term collapses from its maximum amplitude of $0.812^\circ$ ($48.72'$) down to only: $$\Delta L_1 = 0.812^\circ \times 0.0275 \approx +0.0223^\circ = +1.34'$$
