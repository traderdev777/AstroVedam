3.2 Term Formulation & Parameter Definitions

Each coordinate series is evaluated as a Poisson series: $$L(\tau) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} \tau^i \sum_{j=1}^{N_i} A_{i,j} \cos(B_{i,j} + C_{i,j} \tau)$$ $$B(\tau) = \sum_{i=0}^{m} \tau^i \sum_{j=1}^{M_i} A_{i,j} \cos(B_{i,j} + C_{i,j} \tau)$$ $$R(\tau) = \sum_{i=0}^{k} \tau^i \sum_{j=1}^{K_i} A_{i,j} \cos(B_{i,j} + C_{i,j} \tau)$$

Where:

- $\tau = \frac{T}{10} = \frac{\text{JD} - 2451545.0}{365250}$ is time in Julian millennia from J2000.0 ($T$ is Julian centuries). - $A$ is amplitude: in radians for Longitude ($L$) and Latitude ($B$); in Astronomical Units (AU) for Radius ($R$). - $B$ is phase angle in radians ($0 \le B < 2\pi$). - $C$ is frequency in radians per Julian millennium ($\text{rad} / \tau$).

3.3 Spherical-to-Cartesian Coordinate Transformation

The heliocentric rectangular coordinates $(x, y, z)$ in the ecliptic of date are obtained via: $$x = R \cos(L) \cos(B)$$ $$y = R \sin(L) \cos(B)$$ $$z = R \sin(B)$$ $$r = R$$ This transformation is strictly orthogonal, volume-preserving, and mathematically exact.

4.1 Evaluation of Planetary Aberration vs Light-Time

In observational astrometry, the apparent direction of a solar system body at observation time $t$ viewed from an observer moving with velocity $\vec{v}_{\text{Earth}}$ is governed by planetary aberration. By the classical Lorentz transformation for $v/c \ll 1$: $$\vec{u}_{\text{apparent}}(t) \propto \vec{r}_{\text{target}}(t - \tau) - \vec{r}_{\text{observer}}(t)$$ Where $\tau = \frac{|\vec{r}_{\text{target}}(t - \tau) - \vec{r}_{\text{observer}}(t)|}{c}$. Evaluating the target's heliocentric position at the retarded epoch $t - \tau$ relative to Earth's position at observation epoch $t$ automatically incorporates first-order stellar aberration. AstroVedam's implementation follows this exact physical equivalence.

4.2 Equivalence with JPL Horizons Quantity 31

JPL Horizons Quantity 31 is defined as: > "Observer-centered IAU76/80 ecliptic-of-date longitude and latitude of the target centers' apparent position, with light-time, gravitational deflection of light, and stellar aberrations." - Ecliptic Reference Frame: Horizons Quantity 31 references the true ecliptic and equinox of date. AstroVedam evaluates VSOP87D in the mean ecliptic of date and applies IAU 1980 nutation in longitude ($\Delta\psi$) to refer coordinates to the true apparent equinox of date. - Relativistic Gravitational Deflection: Grazing deflection by the Sun at Saturn's opposition or quadrature is $\le 0.004''$ ($0.00007'$), which is negligible. - Equivalence Conclusion: The production code computes the identical astronomical observable as JPL Horizons Quantity 31.
