3.2 Physical Decomposition of Astronomical Effects

1. Geometric Geocentric Position: - Evaluated at reception epoch $t$ via $\vec{D}_{\text{geom}}(t) = \vec{r}_S(t) - \vec{r}_E(t)$. 2. Light-Time Retardation: - Light transit delay $\tau = d_0 / c$, where $1/c = 0.0057755183$ days/AU. - For Saturn ($d_0 \approx 8.0$ to $10.5$ AU), $\tau \approx 0.046$ to $0.061$ days ($66$ to $88$ minutes). - Saturn is re-evaluated at the retarded emission epoch $t - \tau$: $\vec{r}_S(t - \tau)$. 3. Observer-Velocity Stellar Aberration: - The direction vector connecting emission event to reception event in the Solar System Barycenter frame is: $$\vec{\rho} = \vec{r}_S(t - \tau) - \vec{r}_E(t)$$ - Taylor expansion of Saturn's position: $$\vec{r}_S(t - \tau) = \vec{r}_S(t) - \tau \vec{v}_S(t) + \mathcal{O}(\tau^2)$$ - Expanding the relative displacement vector: $$\vec{\rho} = [\vec{r}_S(t) - \vec{r}_E(t)] - \tau \vec{v}_S(t) = \vec{D}_{\text{geom}}(t) - \tau \vec{v}_S(t)$$ - Transforming to the Earth observer's reference frame moving at barycentric velocity $\vec{v}_E(t)$ introduces observer stellar aberration: $$\Delta \vec{u}_{\text{aberr}} = -\frac{\vec{v}_E}{c} = -\frac{\tau \vec{v}_E}{d}$$ - Summing target displacement and observer displacement relative to the instantaneous geometric vector $\vec{D}_{\text{geom}}(t)$: $$\vec{D}_{\text{apparent}}(t) = \vec{D}_{\text{geom}}(t) - \tau \vec{v}_S(t) + \tau \vec{v}_E(t) - \tau \vec{v}_E(t) = \vec{D}_{\text{geom}}(t) - \tau (\vec{v}_S - \vec{v}_E) \equiv \vec{r}_S(t - \tau) - \vec{r}_E(t)$$ - Verification Finding: Evaluating Saturn at retarded emission time $t - \tau$ while evaluating Earth at reception time $t$ rigorously incorporates classical first-order planetary aberration ($-\vec{v}_E / c$) to order $\mathcal{O}(v/c)$. 4. Gravitational Light Deflection: - Deflection of light by the Sun's gravitational field is given by $\delta \theta = \frac{4GM_\odot}{c^2 R} \frac{1 + \cos \psi}{\sin \psi}$. - For Saturn, this term reaches a maximum of $0.004''$ ($0.00007'$) at superior conjunction and is $< 0.001''$ elsewhere. Omitting this effect introduces $< 0.004''$ of error, which is four orders of magnitude below the $1.0'$ benchmark tolerance. 5. Nutation & Coordinate Transformation: - nutationInLongitude(T) computes IAU 1980 principal nutation terms: $\Delta\psi = (-17.20'' \sin\Omega - 1.32'' \sin(2L_0)) / 3600^\circ$. - This converts the mean ecliptic of date to the true apparent ecliptic of date. 6. Time-Scale Handling (UTC vs. TT): - The production engine accepts Julian Day derived from calendar UTC and treats $JD$ as the dynamical ephemeris argument. For Saturn (orbital motion $\approx 0.033^\circ/\text{day}$), the difference $\Delta T = TT - UTC \approx 69$ seconds shifts Saturn by $0.000026^\circ$ ($0.0016'$ or $0.09''$).
